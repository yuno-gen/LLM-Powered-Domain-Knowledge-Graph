The European Union finalized the EU AI Act in 2024, the first comprehensive horizontal regulation governing artificial intelligence systems. The EU AI Act classifies AI systems by risk tier and imposes binding requirements on providers of general-purpose AI models, including OpenAI, Anthropic, Google DeepMind, and Meta. The European Commission established the AI Office to oversee enforcement and coordinate with national regulators.

In October 2023 US President Joe Biden signed Executive Order 14110 on Safe, Secure, and Trustworthy Artificial Intelligence, directing the National Institute of Standards and Technology to publish AI safety guidelines. Executive Order 14110 requires developers of frontier models to share red-team safety results with the US government. The order also created the US AI Safety Institute under NIST.

OpenAI's board removed Sam Altman as CEO in November 2023 in a dispute over governance and safety oversight. Microsoft CEO Satya Nadella publicly backed Altman, and within five days the board reinstated Altman. OpenAI subsequently restructured its board, adding Bret Taylor as chair and Larry Summers as a director, and dissolved its Superalignment team led by Ilya Sutskever and Jan Leike.

The UK AI Safety Institute, established after the Bletchley Park AI Safety Summit hosted by Rishi Sunak in November 2023, partnered with the US AI Safety Institute to coordinate evaluations of frontier models from OpenAI, Anthropic, and Google DeepMind. The Bletchley Declaration was signed by twenty-eight countries including the United States, China, and the European Union.

California Governor Gavin Newsom vetoed Senate Bill 1047 in September 2024 after intense lobbying from OpenAI, Google, and Meta, who argued the bill would have stifled innovation. SB 1047 was authored by State Senator Scott Wiener and would have required developers of frontier models trained with more than ten to the twenty-six floating-point operations to implement kill-switch mechanisms.
